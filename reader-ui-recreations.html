Why it reads as effortless

Vertical scroll gives direct gesture-to-progress mapping — scrolling is reading, and progress is visible through movement. No indirection, no learned mapping, no mode. The feedback is the same physics as every feed the reader already scrolls.

There is exactly one tap zone: the whole screen, and it toggles chrome. No page-turn zones exist because there are no pages. This is the entire reason the format needs no Guided View — it already solved what Guided View was invented to patch.

The tap-zone model everyone copies

Six named layouts, each invertible horizontally, vertically or both. L-shaped is the thumb-reach-optimal one — the forward zone runs along the bottom and right edge, so almost every tap made while holding the phone normally advances.

On opening the reader Mihon flashes a translucent overlay of the current zones, then fades it. That is the best answer found anywhere to the invisible-chrome learnability problem: immersion and discoverability, for about 600 ms once per session. The overlay above is that flash, held on.

Preloads when scroll velocity crosses a threshold — intent-predictive, not distance-based

Two shipped bugs that are free lessons

Anchor zones to the viewport, never the transformed content layer — v0.17.0 fixed zones shifting after zoom-out. And a tap that arrests momentum scroll must be swallowed, not counted as an advance — v0.19.2.

A 25 / 50 / 25 split, and the centre is huge

In vertical mode the quarters sit top and bottom. The centre half is dedicated entirely to toggling chrome — a far larger chrome target than any other reader here, and a deliberate trade: fewer accidental advances, at the cost of a smaller advance zone.

There is no hover reveal. Click centre or press m. This reads as a defect to new users — the project has a bug report literally titled "No webreader settings or progess bar visible."

Worth stealing: the help dialog

Komga never publishes its shortcut list in the docs. It lives only in an in-app dialog on h that shows only the shortcuts valid for the current mode. That context-awareness is a genuinely good idea.

Reading Profiles — the one idea worth copying wholesale

Settings resolve through a real cascade: implicit (device-generated) → series-bound → library-bound → user default, with device-matching profiles winning at each tier. Tweak anything mid-read and it silently creates an implicit profile you can name and keep later.

This is the mature answer to the single loudest cross-venue complaint in the whole research — settings that aren't scoped to the mode they belong to. Komga, MangaDex and KOReader users all hit it independently, and in two of three cases shipped their own userscripts rather than wait.

Auto-detect
Opens webtoon content in webtoon mode automatically — user-reported ~80% hit rate
Prefetch
PREFETCH_PAGES = 10 circular buffer, plus first/last 5 of the adjacent chapter within 10 of a boundary
Scroll
Debounced 20 ms; native scrollend with a 100 ms polling fallback for Safari
Chrome timer
OVERLAY_AUTO_CLOSE_TIME = 3000 ms, suppressed while the settings drawer is open
Page turns
Instant image swap — no animation anywhere in this reader

The bug to design against

Auto-advance doesn't respect already-read state: opening a read chapter in webtoon mode scrolls to the bottom, skips to the next, and if that one is also read, continues endlessly.

The chapter-gap warning

Nothing else surveyed has this. It warns when advancing would skip episodes that simply don't exist yet — exactly right for serialised work published on an irregular cadence, which is what a webcomic is.

And the one setting split done right

"Turn page in long strip mode" is an independent toggle. It disables tap-to-advance in continuous mode without touching page-turning in paged modes. That is the correct shape for a per-mode setting, and MangaDex is the only reader here that got it right.

Which makes the rest more frustrating: image-fit is global across modes, so the setting that makes a long strip readable makes paged content unreadable. The dev acknowledged it — "It is just already quite a lot of options to fit together" — and two years later the community shipped a userscript instead.

What to take for Snadiya

  1. One tap zone, not three. A vertical strip has no pages, so page-turn zones are borrowed complexity. WEBTOON's whole-screen chrome toggle is the honest model. If you add tap-to-advance later, make it a separate toggle the way MangaDex did — never wired to the same setting as paged behaviour.
  2. Never trim whitespace. Gutters are the author's timing instrument — "an endless gutter north and south of any panel." A global crop-borders setting destroys pacing. If you offer trim at all, scope it to paged content only.
  3. Don't impose a background colour behind the strip. Gutter colour is an authored choice; one creator picked black gutters specifically because they read as spooky. A reader-level background picker overrides intent.
  4. Advance by ~3/4 of the viewport, not a full screen. The overlap is what stops readers losing their line. Mihon shipped this deliberately.
  5. Flash the tap zones once on open, then fade. It costs ~600 ms and buys you immersion without the mystery-meat problem that Komga and MangaDex both demonstrably have.
  6. Scope every setting to its mode from day one. This is the loudest unmet need across every venue where real users talk. Kavita's profile cascade is the reference implementation. Retrofitting it is what MangaDex has been unable to do for two years.
  7. Preload bidirectionally and by pixel distance, not page count. Readers re-scroll constantly, and variable panel heights break count-based windows. Prioritise the visible page above prefetch work, and target 0.1 s for the next screen — decoded and resident, not merely downloaded.
  8. Zoom is the universal failure — and your escape hatch. Kavita, Komga, MangaDex and Kindle Cloud Reader all lack working zoom for the same architectural reason: CSS width/height clamping for fit modes kills native browser zoom. An in-canvas transform-based zoom layer sidesteps it entirely, and is the clearest differentiator available in this space.
